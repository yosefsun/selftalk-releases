Built on research, honest about limits

NLP itself has little evidence behind it, so Selftalk doesn't pretend otherwise. It uses what is supported: repetition and priming, believable self-affirmation, talking to yourself in the second person, and “when X, I will Y” plans. Lines are realistic (“I'm becoming bolder every day”), never over-the-top (“I am perfect”), because those can backfire.

It is a self-help reminder tool, not therapy or a medical device. The full reading list ships inside the app.

Safety first

Flashing content can affect people with photosensitive epilepsy, so Selftalk is conservative on purpose.

⚠️

Notice before Flash

The default is a gentle fade. The first time you switch to quick Flash, you must read and accept a short safety notice. It stays in Settings.

⏱️

Never more than one per 10 s

Far below the 3-flashes-a-second limit of accessibility guidelines (WCAG 2.3.1), and no strobing, ever.

🌈

Soft, slow color

Rainbow mode is a slow drift (one cycle takes at least 4 s). Gentle mode fades in and out, and turns on automatically with “Reduce motion”.

Install in a minute

This early version is signed but not yet notarized by Apple, so macOS asks you to confirm the first time.

  1. Download and open the zipDrag Selftalk into your Applications folder.
  2. Open it the first timeDouble-click Selftalk. If macOS says it can't verify the developer, open System Settings → Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway.
  3. Read the safety noticeThen pick a language, a writing style and the goals you care about. Your first reminder appears a moment later.
  4. Find it in the menu barThe ✨ icon pauses, shows one now, or opens Settings. Selftalk also lives in your Dock and ⌘Tab, and keeps itself up to date.
